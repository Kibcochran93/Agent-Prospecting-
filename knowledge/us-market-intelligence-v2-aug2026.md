# 1. Purpose and scope

This document merges two pieces of research. The first, issued 29 July 2026, analyzed public board meeting transcripts obtained through the GovSpend pilot to extract the language US institutions actually use. The second, completed 24 August 2026, examined sector press, association research, federal and state regulation, and published board minutes, and was prompted by a review of the Fall 2026 US newsletter set.
Where the two disagree, the disagreement is recorded rather than resolved by preference. Section 2.3 explains why they disagree and what to do about it. It is the most consequential finding in this version.
Everything here is drawn from primary sources. Institutions are named throughout for internal traceability. Section 9 sets out which items must not appear in customer-facing material and why.

## Method

Two corpora sit behind this document.
- Transcripts. Sixteen full board and committee meeting transcripts, approximately 338,000 words, spanning community colleges, regional public universities, technical universities, and two state systems across eleven states. Meeting dates run from September 2025 to July 2026. Terms were captured verbatim and frequency counted. Full list at Appendix A.
- External sources. Twenty documents comprising federal regulation, state administrative code, sector press, association research, published board minutes, competitor product material, and higher education job postings. Dates run from 2024 to August 2026. Full list at Appendix B.
Financial posture was assigned only where a transcript supports it. Where a claim could not be evidenced it is marked as unknown rather than inferred.

# 2. Headline findings


## 2.1 The term 'Student CRM' does not exist in this market

The phrase appears zero times across 338,000 words and sixteen institutions. In the United States, customer relationship management (CRM) carries a commercial sales connotation, and boards do not use it to describe student-facing systems.
This finding was reached independently a second time from market positioning sources, which place CRM firmly in the recruitment and admissions category, where SEAtS does not compete. Two methods, two corpora, same answer. This is the single highest-priority correction across all US-facing material and is now very well evidenced.

## 2.2 Vendor category language is not customer language

Several terms that dominate the vendor landscape do not appear at board level at all. 'Early alert' appears zero times. 'Timetabling' appears zero times. Institutions describe the same functions using their own operational vocabulary: advising, persistence, success teams, progress checks, and utilization reports.
'Case management' belongs in the same group, and it carries an additional risk because the term does exist in US higher education with a narrower meaning than we intend. It names a job rather than a product category, and that job sits on the crisis and conduct side of student affairs. The established category term for what SEAtS actually does is coordinated care. Section 5.5 sets this out in full.

## 2.3 Practitioner vocabulary is not board vocabulary

This is new in version 2 and it reconciles a direct conflict between the two corpora.
Board transcripts contain zero instances of 'early alert', 'last date of attendance' or 'R2T4'. Sector press and federal regulation use all three freely. The Assistant Vice President for Academic and Student Affairs at APLU refers to 'early alert systems' in an Inside Higher Ed interview. 'Last date of attendance' is the operative term in the federal rule that governs it, and financial aid practitioners abbreviate it to LDA in normal conversation.
Both observations are accurate. They describe different rooms.
- Registrars, financial aid directors, institutional research staff and student success practitioners use this vocabulary daily. It is correct and expected in a data sheet, an RFP response, a compliance conversation, or any exchange with the office that owns the process.
- Trustees and boards do not use it. They discuss the money and the mechanism, not the regulatory shorthand or the vendor category.
The practical rule is to match the vocabulary to the room the document will be read in. A board paper that says "early alert" or "R2T4" reads as vendor language. A conversation with a registrar that avoids "LDA" reads as uninformed. Section 3 sets out which terms belong in which room.

## 2.4 Compliance acronyms are absent even where the mechanics are discussed

'Last date of attendance', 'Return of Title IV Funds' (R2T4), and 'contact hour' each appear zero times in the transcripts. City College of San Francisco spent several minutes of a board meeting explaining the attendance-based funding mechanics that sit directly underneath those terms, without ever using them.
This does not reduce the commercial value of the compliance position. It changes how it is introduced at board level: lead with the money and the mechanism, and introduce the shorthand only once the conversation reaches the office that owns it.

## 2.5 Attendance is revenue in formula-funded states

In California and Texas, state funding is calculated in a way that makes attendance and enrollment reporting accuracy a direct revenue question rather than an administrative one. This remains the strongest commercial finding in the corpus and is set out in full in section 6.

## 2.6 Financial pressure does not remove the compliance conversation

The working assumption going into the first exercise was that institutions under financial pressure will not engage on compliance. The transcripts do not support that in its simple form. A constrained college committed roughly $300,000 per year to two new systems, and a college absorbing an $8 million funding cut responded by commissioning an audit of its own reporting systems. Section 7 sets out the revised rule.

## 2.7 Two funding mechanics moved after version 1 was issued

Texas reduced its outcomes-based funding rates on 22 July 2026, one week before version 1 was circulated, and the federal Return of Title IV Funds rules changed on 1 July 2026. Both affect claims currently in circulation. Section 8 covers them.

## 3.3 Regional variants to respect

- Dual credit versus dual enrollment. 'Dual credit' appears 39 times across six institutions, concentrated in Idaho and Illinois. 'Dual enrollment' appears seven times in one institution, in California. Using the wrong one for the region signals that the material was written elsewhere.
- Part-time is the norm, not the edge case. North Idaho College reported 66 percent of students attending part-time. Joliet Junior College reported that most students are part-time, with 21 percent full-time. Copy that assumes a full-time residential student as the default describes a different market.
- Terms that travel intact. Retention, completion, engagement, and advising are used the same way on both sides. These do not need substitution.

# 4. Language bank: board transcripts

Terms below are verbatim from the transcripts. Attribution is given so claims can be traced.

## 4.1 Scheduling, space, and utilization

This is the richest theme in the corpus and the one closest to an unserved need.
We use a classroom utilization report that shows every hour who's occupied, not who, if that room is being occupied.
Joliet Junior College, academic administrator, 28 January 2026
- 'Space utilization' appears nine times across three institutions. City College of San Francisco calculates it per location on a state Chancellor's Office formula and stated on the record that the entire college is underutilized by that measure, as are most community colleges, because of how the formula works.
- Joliet reviews by hand every section that is under 50 percent filled. The deans and the academic vice president spent roughly five hours on this in the week before term started.
- Joliet on afternoon sections: students do not enroll in them, and running them anyway 'becomes fiscally irresponsible'.
- A Joliet trustee asked, unprompted in open session, whether the college had done a utilization study to confirm every classroom is being used. That question came from the board, not from administration.
- North Idaho College described what it expects from its scheduling system: identifying faculty load, course loads, where there is a wait list, and where students want to take classes by time and date.
- Washtenaw Community College opened a ten-year master plan citing 'tighter resources' and 'changing enrollment patterns' as the pressures 'reshaping how campuses use their space'.

### Scheduling as a retention decision

The most useful single passage in the corpus for positioning SEAtS Schedule alongside student success came from a dean at MSU Denver, in response to a trustee who said that what she had been reading about retention 'has to do with scheduling'.
When we look at schedules in prime time, we're looking at students who we want to attract and get into the pipeline. Put those classes on the edges of the schedule. Fill the middle with those lower division classes.
MSU Denver, academic dean, September 2025
The same dean described asking advisers to be physically scheduled near where students already are, so students can meet them before class, and raised block scheduling as an open question. This is a US institution treating the schedule as a recruitment and retention instrument rather than a room-booking exercise.

## 4.2 Persistence and student success

Calbright College, California's online community college, has built persistence into its organizational structure. It refers to a standing 'Persistence team' that receives and reviews student requests.
Pace and progress timelines were super successful and we saw increased persistence from providing this structure, but the format of them was limited. They were static, sent by email.
Calbright College, March 2026
Calbright is replacing static email pacing with an in-product view where progress updates in real time and students can request a timeline change that routes to the persistence team. That is a US institution describing, in its own words and without prompting, the gap between an email nudge and an instrumented engagement view.
- Joliet Junior College: 1,481 students attended new student advising in fall 2025 and 1,372 enrolled, a 93 percent conversion rate. The college tracks the same cohort into the following term and reports on whether they are persisting.
- MSU Denver: tracking to its highest first-time retention on record at 68.7 percent, against a stated goal of 80 percent from year one to year two. Every department carries an objective or key performance indicator tied to enrollment, retention, graduation rates, or post-graduate outcomes.
- Oregon Tech: integrated advising and retention with admissions and now views the funnel on an 18-month cycle rather than the six-month cycle it says most institutions use.
- Cerritos College: 'success teams', 'success coaches', 'faculty data coaches', and interventions designed to help students stay on track.
- Joliet: the strategic enrollment management (SEM) plan is on its fifth iteration, first launched in 2014. SEM is an established internal framework at community colleges, not a new concept to introduce.

## 4.3 Compliance and accreditation

- Western Carolina University confirmed that the Southern Association of Colleges and Schools Commission on Colleges (SACSCOC) has 73 core principles and standards, runs reaffirmation on a ten-year cycle, and is renaming itself the Commission on Colleges and Universities with effect from 1 September. Their director of assessment and accreditation stated that accreditors examine student achievement metrics, graduation rates, retention rates, whether distance education students have equivalent access to resources, and what student support services exist.
- Cerritos College is in its Institutional Self-Evaluation Report (ISER) cycle with the Accrediting Commission for Community and Junior Colleges (ACCJC), which has reduced its standards from 134 to 30.
- North Idaho College, recently returned to good standing with the Northwest Commission on Colleges and Universities (NWCCU), described accreditation work now proceeding 'as part of a normal institutional process rather than crisis response'.
- University of Oregon renamed its decennial program review to 'program assessment' and added three-year and five-year progress checks explicitly for 'early detection of areas of concern'. It introduced student achievement goals covering retention, graduation rates, and post-graduate outcomes 'mostly in response to new accreditation standards'.

## 4.4 Budget and cost

Joliet Junior College published its full operating expenditure breakdown to the board. This is the most precise figure in the corpus and should replace any estimate currently in circulation.
Software sits inside that 11 percent alongside travel, supplies, and every other contract. It is not an 11 to 20 percent category in its own right. This makes the argument stronger, not weaker: any software purchase is competing for a very thin slice against everything else the college buys.
Phrases worth using because institutions use them:
- 'Enrollment cliff'. Appears five times across three institutions. Joliet has been using it internally for four years. It is a shared reference point, not a novel framing.
- Joliet on procurement: new initiatives are prioritized and funded either by new revenues or by expense reductions. That is the gate any proposal has to clear.
- Joliet: 'we're entering a more constrained operating budget' and 'our budget is driven by personnel and fixed costs which limits discretionary'.
- North Idaho College: 'we've already cut out the easy stuff'. Remaining cuts are 'a harder truth', 'more visible', with 'a greater impact on our programming'.
- University of Central Florida, on the limit of efficiency as a strategy: 'there is a point where efficiency alone cannot sustain continued excellence'.

# 5. Language bank: sector and board papers

This section is new in version 2. It records vocabulary from published sources rather than transcripts, and is therefore subject to the register rule in 2.3. These terms are correct in writing aimed at practitioners, in longer-form content, and in conversation with the office that owns the function. They are not board-meeting vocabulary.

## 5.1 Student success and advising

- Stop-outs. Students who have left but are not framed as permanently gone. Used in national persistence reporting.
- First-year persistence. Persistence describes the student continuing, retention describes the institution's rate. Both corpora keep these apart.
- Data-informed advising. The approved phrase for analytics in advising. Carries less black-box baggage than predictive analytics.
- Basic needs. Housing, food, childcare. A mainstream student success category.
- CARE team. The standing cross-department group that receives student-of-concern referrals. Corroborates the transcript finding of success teams and aligns with case management.
- Gateway courses. Entry-level English and math, which several state funding formulas measure completion against.

## 5.2 Finance and governance

- Net tuition revenue. Tuition after discounting. The CFO's unit. 'Revenue per student' is our phrase, not theirs.
- Attrition. Finance says attrition where student affairs says retention.
- Primary reserve ratio, viability ratio. Standard board financial dashboard measures.
- Admit, yield and retention. The triad every enrollment report to a board is built on.
- Aspirational peers. The comparison set an institution wants to be measured against.
- Institutional effectiveness. A named office with its own AVP, distinct from institutional research. Owns accreditation evidence.
Improving attrition improves enrollment levels in a less costly way than recruiting new students.
Association of Governing Boards, Key Financial Metrics in Higher Education
That sentence is the entire CFO argument, already made by the association that advises their board. Quoting AGB is stronger than asserting it ourselves.

## 5.3 Information technology

- Digital sprawl. The named problem in 2026 IT strategy. EDUCAUSE reports 77 percent of US technology decision-makers seeing moderate to extensive levels.
- Tech stack rationalization. The consolidation project itself. One published case moved from twenty-nine systems to five.
- Doing less with more. A deliberate inversion of the familiar phrase. Fewer systems, used harder.

## 5.4 State frameworks

- Student Throughline. The Massachusetts Board of Higher Education framework, running recruitment to enrollment to retention to completion to employment. A ready-made map of where SEAtS sits, in the state's own words.
- Academic momentum. A named pillar of Massachusetts SUCCESS 2.0, alongside case management and financial wraparound supports. Case management being state policy language makes that story policy-aligned in Massachusetts rather than a product feature.
- Programs of Strategic Emphasis, Metric 10. Florida's designated priority fields, and the Board of Trustees Choice metric in its performance model. Each institution picks its own Metric 10, so ask which one a Florida prospect chose.

## 5.5 Coordinated care, and what to do with case management

New in version 2, and the most direct product-naming finding in either version.
"Case management" is not a neutral category label in the United States. It is a job title, and the job sits on the crisis and conduct side of student affairs. Current postings include Critical Incidents and Student Support Case Manager and Case Manager for Student Assistance. Boise State publishes an official job standard for "Case Manager/Care Coordinator". The University of Michigan School of Social Work lists these roles. The professional body publishing on the practice is NABITA, the behavioral intervention team association, and the practitioner literature records that the field emerged in close connection with behavioral intervention teams, threat assessment and mental health concerns following high-profile campus incidents.
The scope does extend to academic support and retention, so the term is not inaccurate. The risk is narrower than that: a US reader encountering Connected Case Management has reasonable grounds to read it as students of concern, conduct and threat assessment rather than everyday support coordination. That is a smaller and more loaded category than the one we sell into.

### The established category is coordinated care

EAB, whose Navigate360 is the incumbent in this category and which appears in the transcript corpus at Cerritos College and PASSHE, maintains a glossary entry for coordinated care. A glossary entry indicates settled category vocabulary rather than a vendor coinage. Their definition:
a campus-wide approach to student support that helps colleges and universities identify student needs earlier, connect students to the right resources faster, and make sure someone follows through.
EAB, Coordinated Care in Higher Education
EAB also describes it as "a student success operating model" that "aligns people, processes, technology, and data so campus teams can work together around shared student needs".
The overlap with existing SEAtS copy is close enough to be worth noting. Among the problems EAB lists coordinated care as solving is that students repeat their circumstances across multiple offices. The customer newsletter headline is that students should not have to tell the same story twice. The pitch is already aligned to the category; only the label sits outside it.

### Keep case as the unit of work

EAB places case management inside coordinated care as a component, describing case management documentation of "ownership, next steps, and status" alongside structured referral workflows. They also use "case closure" as an operational metric. Case, case owner and case closure are therefore correct and current at the level of the individual piece of work. The change is to the category name above them, not to the mechanism beneath.

### Terms worth adopting from this source

- Coordinated care. The category. Also appears as live program naming at San Diego State (Coordinated Care Advising) and UMBC.
- Coordinated Care Network. EAB's 2026 framing for connecting multiple campus offices.
- Triage rules, clear ownership, and repeatable outreach. A close description of what Action Centers do, in the incumbent’s own words.
- Case closure. An operational metric, used alongside faculty response rates and service utilization.
- DFWI trends. D, F, Withdrawal and Incomplete rates. A standard measure not previously captured in either version.
- Unified student profiles. Consolidated data from multiple systems. The phrase the market uses for what we describe as a single view.

### Two qualifications

Massachusetts is a genuine exception. As recorded at 5.4, the Board of Higher Education names case management as a pillar of its SUCCESS 2.0 program. In that state the term is state policy language and should be used as written.
This is EAB vocabulary. Adopting it makes us legible inside their category rather than differentiated from it. The differentiator then has to carry the weight: our signal is verified attendance, which the incumbent does not hold.

# 6. Evidence anchors

Four findings are strong enough to build campaign material around. All need care in how they are used.

## 6.1 Attendance is revenue in formula-funded states

City College of San Francisco explained to its board how full-time equivalent students (FTES) are counted, because the college is deciding whether to close a campus:
- Credit courses generate FTES from census dates. Non-credit is daily attendance.
- Non-credit online now uses what the state calls a two-census method, collected at two points in the term and averaged.
- Non-credit is open entry, open exit, so the college has to 'accumulate the hours that the students were in attendance'.
- Attendance is no longer captured on paper. Faculty mark it in the learning management system.
- A trustee corrected the record to note that positive attendance is not exclusive to non-credit, and also applies to credit sections that do not meet on a regular schedule.
- The Student Centered Funding Formula is roughly three quarters of the college's unrestricted revenue.
El Paso Community College is absorbing an $8 million reduction against a $35.9 million state appropriation, described in the meeting as 'a hard hit coming out of nowhere'. Texas has moved to a fully outcomes-based funding model. The college's response, stated in the opening minutes of its budget presentation, was to engage its internal auditor to review the college's enrollment reporting systems.
Internally, we have engaged our internal auditor to review our enrollment reporting systems to assure our reporting is accurate and to maximize what we are supposed to receive.
El Paso Community College, Vice President of Finance and Administration, 25 June 2026
A college losing $8 million bought assurance on its own reporting accuracy. That is the clearest evidence in the corpus that in formula-funded states the cost message and the reporting accuracy message are the same message.
Qualification before this is used nationally. The mechanic is explicit in California and Texas. Whether Missouri, Kansas, Oklahoma, Nebraska, and Arkansas use attendance-linked or outcomes-linked formulas has not yet been verified. This remains open and is carried forward as step 2 in section 10.

## 6.2 Regular and substantive interaction

Regular and substantive interaction (RSI) is the federal standard that separates distance education from correspondence education. A course in which the instructor only responds to student-initiated contact is treated as correspondence, which carries worse federal aid treatment. Accreditors audit it.
A well-resourced California community college disclosed the following to its board:
- Its accreditor samples roughly 75 to 100 courses at random and requires 85 percent to demonstrate RSI correctly.
- First internal audit: 59 percent passing. The presenters' word for the reaction was alarm bells.
- Second audit: 79 percent. Still short. A third was underway at the time of the meeting.
- Over 700 faculty completed RSI training in fall 2025. The centre for teaching excellence sends weekly RSI reminders.
The point is not that the college is careless. It trained 700 faculty, built the training, ran repeat audits, and sends weekly reminders, and still sat at 79 percent. The gap is evidence and measurement, not effort or awareness. That is the message.
Two hard limits on how this is used. First, SEAtS is not an RSI compliance product and must not be presented as one. RSI concerns faculty-initiated, substantive, recurring contact inside online courses. SEAtS measures attendance and engagement and surfaces flags. The overlap is on the evidence side only. Second, the college presenter attributed RSI to 'Title V'. That attribution is wrong. The requirement sits in the federal definition of distance education tied to Title IV aid eligibility and is enforced through accreditor review. Do not repeat the Title V attribution.

## 6.3 The Return of Title IV fourteen-day clock

New in version 2, and not present in the transcript corpus because the rules took effect after most of those meetings.
Under the R2T4 rules effective 1 July 2026, an institution must document the date of its determination that a student withdrew no later than fourteen days after that student’s last date of attendance. It then has up to thirty days to complete the calculation and forty-five days to return funds. The rules also eliminate R2T4 freeze dates for modules and add an optional exemption allowing a school to treat a student as never having begun attendance if it returns all Title IV funds and cancels institutional charges.
Two things make this commercially useful. The clock starts at last date of attendance, which makes it an attendance-data problem before it is a reporting one. And R2T4 findings have sat in the Department of Education’s top ten compliance findings for years, which practitioners attribute to the complexity of a calculation that looks simple on paper. This is a recurring, evidenced exposure rather than a new risk being introduced.
Register note. Per 2.3, introduce this to a board as the money and the mechanism. The acronym belongs in conversation with the registrar or financial aid office.

## 6.4 Boards are asking for retention data they do not receive

At the University of Tennessee's Education, Research and Service Committee in October 2024, trustees were shown a record first-year retention rate and asked how transfers within the system should be reflected in the retention figures, and how campuses compare against aspirational peers. At the same committee in February 2026 they asked again and went further, encouraging future reports to include second, third and fourth-year retention alongside graduation outcomes. Leadership committed on the record that additional longitudinal retention data would be incorporated into future board reports.
This is a commitment made to a board, in public, to produce reporting the institution does not currently produce. It is a more specific opener than a general retention message, it is verifiable in an institution’s own published minutes, and it lands on reporting burden, which is where the operational efficiency case sits. Worth pulling the last two years of academic or student success committee minutes before any board-facing conversation.

# 7. Segmentation and message selection

The original hypothesis was binary: institutions under financial pressure want a cost message, well-funded institutions will accept a compliance message. The transcripts support the second half and complicate the first.
- North Idaho College is cutting harder than almost any institution in the set and still committed approximately $300,000 per year to two new systems. Every line in its budget presentation was tagged to a numbered strategic plan goal.
- El Paso Community College is losing $8 million and bought an audit of its reporting systems in response.
- Cerritos College is not under budget pressure and gave significant board time to a federal compliance requirement it is currently failing.

## The revised rule

Institutions do not reject compliance messaging. They reject compliance framed as an obligation. What they require is that the spend maps either to a goal the board has already approved, or to revenue the institution is currently leaving on the table. Untagged spend does not clear the gate.

## Posture by institution

Cerritos posture is inferred partly from the absence of cost-cutting discussion in a meeting where the budget sat on the consent agenda. Treat as medium confidence.

# 8. Market movement since version 1

Four developments affect claims currently in circulation.

## 8.1 Texas reduced its outcomes funding rates

On 22 July 2026, one week before version 1 was circulated, the Texas Higher Education Coordinating Board reduced the House Bill 8 outcome reward amounts. The mechanism matters because it inverts the usual argument. Community colleges across the state beat their outcome projections, and because distributions sit under a cap of roughly $1.2 billion until the next legislative session, THECB could not fund the bonuses at the promised level and trimmed them instead, cutting hardest on the incentives for graduating low-income and adult students.
Nearly a dozen colleges face state funding reductions of at least 15 percent in FY27. Texarkana College is down around 25 percent. Tarrant County College is down $4.1 million. Paris Junior College is down $1.4 million despite growing enrollment 20 percent. Collin College is one of the few gaining, up 7.1 percent.
Successful colleges should not be penalized for exceeding expectations.
Stephen Benson, President, Paris Junior College
There is also more nuance to the per-outcome values than we previously had. Transfer and dual-credit outcomes are $3,500, but credentials vary by type and a certificate is $1,750, which is what a large share of completers actually earn. The weights are unchanged at adult learners plus 50 percent and economically or academically disadvantaged plus 25 percent. The FY27 rates are not published on the open web; they sit in the FY 2027 Funding Runs and Funding Model on the THECB Formula Funding page.
How this changes the argument. Producing more outcomes no longer straightforwardly earns more money, and a college that has just lost funding while growing will notice if we say otherwise. The stronger framing is that the pool is fixed and the whole sector has improved, so a college’s allocation depends on its performance relative to every other district. Standing still loses share. That framing does not depend on a rate which may move again.

## 8.2 Outcomes-based funding is contested research

Twenty-nine states run an outcomes model for two-year institutions and twenty-eight for four-year, so the scale is real. MDRC finds these models have had limited effects on credential attainment, particularly for associate and bachelor degrees. An institutional research director may well know this.
Our case never depended on the policy working at state level. It depends on the institution being paid against a metric it has to hit regardless, and MDRC’s own conclusion that outcomes funding needs additional tools is an argument for our category. The adjustment is to say the money sits on the metric, not that the metric is working.

## 8.3 The incumbent SIS already sells an AI assistant

Ellucian sells an AI Virtual Student Assistant on claims of always-on support, 24/7 availability and reduced call and ticket volume, with a tagline of "Empower Students. Unburden Your Staff." A number of prospects run Banner, so to those readers "answers routine questions and escalates the rest" will sound familiar rather than new.
What is distinctive is narrower and harder to match: the agent sits inside Microsoft Teams and reasons over each student’s schedule, attendance and support history, then hands off with that context intact. Lead with the data it can see and where it already lives.

## 8.4 Campus safety has two distinct federal obligations

A Timely Warning covers a Clery crime in Clery Geography representing a serious or continuing threat, usually distributed by email. An Emergency Notification is the mass-alert obligation for a confirmed immediate threat. Safety staff hear the difference immediately.
Describing our product as mass alerts places it in Emergency Notification territory, where Everbridge and Rave are established and integrated. One-tap SOS with live location and journey sharing, delivered in the app students already have, is a duty-of-care story and a stronger one.

# 9. Systems named by institutions

Board minutes name vendors inconsistently, usually only when a line item is discussed rather than consented. The absence of a vendor name is not evidence that no vendor exists.
Three observations for product. First, North Idaho College stated on the record that Coursedog and Element451 together cost approximately $300,000 per year ongoing, and reported implementation difficulty with Coursedog where legacy scheduling practices such as stacked sections could not be represented in the system. Second, at City College of San Francisco the incumbent in the attendance workflow is the learning management system, not a dedicated attendance product. Third, from outside the corpus, Ellucian competes directly in AI student support and Everbridge and Rave in emergency notification. Neither surfaced in the transcripts, which reflects what boards discuss rather than what campuses run.

# 10. What not to say

- Do not use 'Student CRM' in any customer-facing US material, in any context.
- Do not use 'early alert', 'R2T4', 'LDA' or 'last date of attendance' in board-facing material. They are correct with practitioners and absent from every board transcript in the corpus. See 2.3 and 3.2.
- Do not name Cerritos College, or any institution, in connection with a compliance shortfall in published material. It is public record and legally usable, but publishing a named college’s live compliance gap will close doors across an entire state system, and California community colleges are closely networked. Use the anonymized form: ‘one California community college’s internal audits found compliance well short of its accreditor’s threshold despite training over 700 faculty’.
- Do not present SEAtS as a regular and substantive interaction compliance solution. The honest framing is adjacency on the evidence side.
- Do not repeat the 'Title V' attribution for RSI. It is incorrect.
- Do not claim software is 11 to 20 percent of an institutional budget. The evidenced figure is that all non-personnel supplies, contractual spend, and travel together are 11 percent.
- Do not extend the attendance-as-revenue finding beyond California and Texas until the funding formulas in the target states have been checked.
- Do not quote a per-outcome dollar figure for Texas HB 8 until the FY 2027 funding model has been pulled from THECB. The previously used $3,500 is both out of date and, applied to credentials generally, imprecise.
- Do not imply that outcomes-based funding formulas themselves drive improvement. The research does not support it. Say the money sits on the metric.
- Do not use "case management" as a category name in US material, with the single exception of Massachusetts, where it is state policy language. Use coordinated care. Case, case owner and case closure remain correct for the individual unit of work. See 5.5.
- Do not describe campus safety features as mass alerts unless we mean the Clery Emergency Notification obligation specifically.
- Do not attach percentages or industry averages to any claim that cannot be sourced to a named document.

# 11. Recommended next steps

- Apply the vocabulary substitutions in section 3 to existing US-facing material before any new material is produced. This remains the highest-value and lowest-cost action available. The Fall 2026 newsletter set has been reviewed against it and updated drafts exist for the two editions carrying out-of-date Texas figures.
- Confirm whether Arkansas, Missouri, Oklahoma, Kansas, and Nebraska operate attendance-linked or outcomes-linked funding formulas. This determines whether section 6.1 becomes national copy or stays a California and Texas story. Carried forward from version 1 and still open.
- Pull the FY 2027 Funding Runs and Funding Model from THECB so that any Texas figure we publish is current.
- Pull the underlying federal regulation text for distance education and regular and substantive interaction, so that any data sheet citation is sourced rather than paraphrased from a meeting transcript.
