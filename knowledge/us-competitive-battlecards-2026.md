EAB Navigate360 DIRECT • STUDENT SUCCESS | VERY HIGH

[TABLE 6]
LIKELY BUYERS Provost; VP Student Success; Advising leadership; Enrollment/Retention; CIO | WHEN THEY APPEAR Enterprise student-success CRM, advising modernization, retention and coordinated-care programs.

[TABLE 7]
• Large U.S. higher-ed footprint: EAB states 850+ two- and four-year institutions and 95%+ annual renewal. • Deep advising workflow: appointments, campaigns, alerts, referrals, communications and coordinated care. • Strong peer-network / research narrative through EAB’s Student Success Collaborative. | • Lead on operational breadth: attendance + engagement + case management + compliance + scheduling + smart space on one platform. • Make attendance an actionable signal, not a separate workflow: detect → create case → route action → evidence outcome. • Use Microsoft-native architecture and campus-operations consolidation where those matter.

[TABLE 8]
CORE TALK TRACK  “Navigate360 is a strong advising CRM. The question is whether you need another advising system—or a connected operating layer that turns attendance, engagement, compliance and campus data into action across the institution.”

[TABLE 9]
• Is this project only about advising, or are attendance/compliance/scheduling systems also in scope? • How quickly do advisors see changes in actual attendance and engagement? • How many separate platforms are involved from signal detection to intervention? • Is Microsoft/Azure alignment an architectural priority? | • Do not attack EAB’s installed base; reframe the decision around scope and consolidation. • Do not say Navigate360 lacks attendance—focus on SEAtS depth and linkage to broader operations. • Ask for apples-to-apples five-year TCO including any systems that could be retired.

[TABLE 10]
WIN SIGNALS  Attendance/compliance/scheduling included; multi-department sponsor; desire to consolidate systems. | LOSS / RISK SIGNALS  Pure advising RFP; buyer heavily values EAB peer network; incumbent Navigate360 with strong adoption.

Watermark Student Success & Engagement DIRECT • STUDENT SUCCESS | HIGH

[TABLE 12]
LIKELY BUYERS Student Success; Advising; Institutional Effectiveness; Retention; CIO | WHEN THEY APPEAR Retention, predictive risk, early alert, advising, engagement and intervention management.

[TABLE 13]
• Predictive models and early alerts aimed at identifying students who need support. • Advising workflows, appointment scheduling, messaging, campaigns and mobile student experience. • Broad SIS/LMS connectivity story and strong institutional-effectiveness adjacency. | • Differentiate with real attendance/engagement capture plus downstream action. • Expand the buying lens beyond retention software to compliance, scheduling and smart-campus operations. • Show a single operational record connecting what happens in class and on campus to support actions.

Ask how it is captured, validated and tied to workflows. • Separate vendor-published outcome claims from independently verified results. • Probe packaging/tier requirements for Predict, campaigns, texting and advanced capabilities.

[TABLE 16]
WIN SIGNALS  Need to connect behavioral signals to action; consolidation opportunity; attendance pain. | LOSS / RISK SIGNALS  Buyer wants a focused, established retention/advising product and values Watermark suite adjacency.

[TABLE 24]
LIKELY BUYERS CIO; Registrar; Advising; Student Success; Banner/Colleague stakeholders | WHEN THEY APPEAR Advising and retention modernization, especially where Ellucian is already the core SIS provider.

[TABLE 25]

[TABLE 26]
CORE TALK TRACK  “The incumbent argument is powerful—but the real question is whether the SIS-adjacent tool covers the operational workflows the institution is trying to modernize. SEAtS sits across those workflows without requiring the institution to change its SIS.”

[TABLE 27]
• Is the institution trying to simplify vendors—or simplify workflows? • Which capabilities would still require separate products after CRM Advise? • How much custom work is required to connect attendance, compliance and scheduling data? • Who owns the business case: IT, advising, registrar or operations? | • Do not lead with 'best of breed' as a generic claim; tie it to specific requirements. • Expect 'we already own Ellucian' objections—respond with scope/TCO, not feature dumping. • Validate current Ellucian packaging because product portfolio can evolve.

[TABLE 28]
WIN SIGNALS  Cross-functional scope beyond advising; need to remain SIS-independent; consolidation across ops. | LOSS / RISK SIGNALS  Strong Ellucian standardization policy; narrow advising requirement; existing license economics dominate.

[TABLE 30]

[TABLE 31]

[TABLE 34]
WIN SIGNALS  Buyer wants purpose-built operations, quick deployment, Microsoft-native alignment, multiple non-CRM modules. | LOSS / RISK SIGNALS  Salesforce is mandated enterprise platform; strong internal COE; broad CRM transformation budget.

[TABLE 42]

[TABLE 43]

[TABLE 46]
WIN SIGNALS  Consolidation across Registrar/Facilities/Student Success; actual-use analytics matter. | LOSS / RISK SIGNALS  Pure scheduling optimization RFP with mature Series25 deployment and limited adjacent needs.
