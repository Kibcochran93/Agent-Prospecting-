## Checkpoint 2: Trigger research

### Strongest slice

**Growth-stage regional public universities actively implementing measurable retention plans.**

This slice has stronger dated evidence than institutions merely listing student success in an older strategic plan. The clearest accounts are:

1. **University of Tennessee at Chattanooga**
   - Fall 2025 headcount: **12,060**
   - Retention rose to **73.4%**
   - Launched implementation of its Strategic Enrollment Plan on **January 13, 2026**
   - Target: **80% first-year retention by 2030**
   - Known incumbent: **EAB Navigate**
   - Play: not advising replacement. Lead with connected operational data, cross-campus action, and platform consolidation. 

2. **Louisiana State University Shreveport**
   - Fall 2025 headcount: **11,359**
   - On **April 9, 2026**, reported second-year retention above 72%, following redesigned support systems
   - Strategic plan calls for centralized advising, an alert system, streamlined communications, and a five-point retention increase
   - Incumbent platform: **unverified**
   - Play: scale and connect a successful redesign rather than sell foundational student-success software. 

3. **University of Arkansas at Little Rock**
   - Fall 2025 headcount: **8,026**
   - On **September 5, 2025**, reported its largest undergraduate increase since 2001, with strong continuing-freshman and sophomore growth
   - Strategic plan embeds student-success analytics and interventions into departmental reporting
   - Incumbent platform: **unverified**
   - Play: help preserve momentum by making progress checks and intervention coordination more consistent. 

4. **Murray State University**
   - Fall 2025 headcount: **9,932**
   - On **October 29, 2025**, reported 78% first-to-second-year retention, its highest in five years
   - Spring 2026 headcount later fell 4.3%, while year-over-year retention remained 78.1%
   - Possible EAB use appears in older material, but current incumbent is **unverified**
   - Play: maintaining gains while enrollment conditions shift. 

**Conclusion:** UTC has the strongest active implementation trigger. LSU Shreveport has the strongest evidence of a successful redesign that may now need scaling.

## Checkpoint 4: Buying group

All four are regional public universities, so the institutional buying-group model is consistent.

| Role | Likely offices/titles | Problem owned |
|---|---|---|
| Economic buyer | Provost; Vice President/Chancellor for Student Affairs or Student Success | Persistence, completion and institutional priorities |
| Operational owner | Student Success; Retention; Advising; Enrollment Management | Progress checks, intervention and coordinated execution |
| Technical owner | Chief Information Officer; enrollment technology leader | Student Information System, Learning Management System, integrations and access |
| Evidence stakeholder | Institutional Research/Effectiveness | Cohort definitions, measures and intervention outcomes |
| Likely blocker | Owner of the incumbent platform or existing advising process | Adoption, duplication and replacement concerns |

### Account-specific starting points

- **UTC:** Enrollment Management and Student Affairs or Student Success first; provost second. IT and the EAB Navigate owner must enter early because this is an incumbent-expansion play.
- **LSU Shreveport:** Student Success first; Academic Affairs second. Its redesigned support model makes the operational owner the most credible entry.
- **UA Little Rock:** Student Affairs or Academic Affairs Retention first; provost second. Institutional Research should validate how analytics reach departmental action.
- **Murray State:** Student Affairs and Enrollment Management first; provost second. Verify the current EAB relationship before positioning.

**Outreach order:** operational owner, economic buyer, Institutional Research, then IT. Each office requires distinct copy. Do not send one message to multiple offices at the same institution.

## Louisiana State University Shreveport

### Day 1  
**Subject:** Scaling what LSUS fixed

LSUS has already shown that redesigning student support can materially improve second- and third-year persistence.

The next question is whether that model can scale as enrollment passes 11,000 without losing the individualized support behind the gains.

How are you measuring that?

Kib

### Day 4  
**Subject:** The students balancing three jobs

LSUS describes a student body in which work, family and academics often compete for the same hours.

A missed pattern may therefore matter more than a single missed class. Can success teams see that pattern while there’s still time to intervene?

Kib

### Day 8  
**Subject:** A readiness exercise, not a reset

I’m not suggesting LSUS replace the support framework that is producing results.

We use a short readiness exercise to identify which attendance, engagement and advising data reach success teams, where delays remain, and what should happen automatically versus through staff judgment.

Would that be useful?

Kib

### Day 13  
**Subject:** Where this sits at LSUS

Does the next phase of the redesign belong with Student Success, Academic Affairs or the team centralizing advising?

I’d rather direct the question accurately than assume the organization chart tells the whole story.

Kib

### Day 20  
**Subject:** Keeping the gains intact

The important result at LSUS is not simply record enrollment. It’s that more students are staying.

I’ll stop here, but the scaling question remains worth revisiting as the centralized model develops.

Kib
